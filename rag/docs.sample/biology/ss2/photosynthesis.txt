Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants and some other organisms
manufacture their own food (glucose) from carbon dioxide and water, using light
energy trapped by the green pigment chlorophyll, and releasing oxygen as a
by-product. It takes place mainly in the leaves, specifically in cell organelles
called chloroplasts.

The word equation for photosynthesis is:
carbon dioxide + water --(light energy, chlorophyll)--> glucose + oxygen.

The balanced chemical equation is:
6CO2 + 6H2O --> C6H12O6 + 6O2.

Conditions necessary for photosynthesis:
1. Light - provides the energy that drives the reaction (sunlight is the natural
   source).
2. Chlorophyll - the green pigment in the chloroplasts that absorbs light energy.
3. Carbon dioxide - a raw material taken in from the air through the stomata.
4. Water - a raw material absorbed from the soil by the roots.
A suitable temperature is also needed for the enzymes that control the reaction.

Importance of photosynthesis:
Plants are producers; they form the base of nearly every food chain, so almost
all living things depend on photosynthesis for food. The process also releases
oxygen for respiration and removes carbon dioxide from the atmosphere.
